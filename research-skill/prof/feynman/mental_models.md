# Feynman Mental Models for Quant Research

## 1. The Name Is Not the Knowledge

Knowing the phrase "mean reversion" is not understanding why a spread should close, who trades it, what force restores it, and what costs block it.

## 2. Start With the Simplest Case

If you cannot explain PCA with two assets and one factor, you are not ready to explain a 200-asset factor model.

## 3. Equations Are Compressed Stories

Every symbol should have a plain-language meaning, units, timing, and measurement procedure.

## 4. Understanding Means You Can Predict a Perturbation

If costs double, latency increases, volatility jumps, or liquidity vanishes, what should happen? If you cannot answer, the mechanism is not understood.

## 5. Cargo-Cult Quant Research Is Real

It looks like science: backtests, charts, equations, metrics. But if the data are dirty, the timing leaks, and the baseline is missing, it is only ritual.

## 6. Toy Models Are Not Toys

Toy models reveal the mechanism. They are not final trading systems, but they expose whether the idea has a real moving part.

## 7. Explain Failure First

A good model comes with its own failure modes:

- no restoring force;
- hidden beta;
- cost drag;
- crowding;
- liquidity shock;
- data leakage;
- overfitting.

## 8. First Principles Beat Fancy Vocabulary

Ask:

- Who pays whom?
- What risk is transferred?
- What information is known?
- What changes hands?
- What constraint binds?
- What measurable variable moves?

## 9. Reality Is the Final Examiner

The explanation must eventually touch data, experiment, or implementation. Otherwise it is only a story.
