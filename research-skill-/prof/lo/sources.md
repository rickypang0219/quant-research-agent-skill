- **The Adaptive Markets Hypothesis: Market Efficiency from an Evolutionary Perspective** — Andrew W. Lo, Journal of Portfolio Management, 2004.  
  https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/JPM2004_Pub.pdf

## 3. Financial economics, empirical finance, and market efficiency

### Empirical finance textbook

- **The Econometrics of Financial Markets** — John Y. Campbell, Andrew W. Lo, and A. Craig MacKinlay, Princeton University Press, 1997.  
  Official MIT book listing: https://alo.mit.edu/types/book/  
  Google Books summary: https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Econometrics_of_Financial_Markets.html?id=lkeKhnqUHx8C  
  Covers return predictability, Random Walk tests, market microstructure, event studies, CAPM, APT, term structure, ARCH, neural networks, statistical fractals, and chaos.

### Random walk and predictability

- **A Non-Random Walk Down Wall Street** — Andrew W. Lo and A. Craig MacKinlay, Princeton University Press, 1999.  
  Official MIT book listing: https://alo.mit.edu/types/book/  
  JSTOR listing: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7tccx  
  Use for empirical challenge to pure random-walk thinking and for predictability testing.

- **Long-Term Memory in Stock Market Prices** — Andrew W. Lo, Econometrica, 1991.  
  JSTOR: https://www.jstor.org/stable/2938368  
  SSRN: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=463442  
  Use for robust testing of long-run dependence and distinction between short-range and long-range dependence.

- **Stock Market Prices Do Not Follow Random Walks: Evidence from a Simple Specification Test** — Lo and MacKinlay, 1987/1988 working paper and later publications.  
  NBER listing appears in Lo/RePEc bibliographies.

### Technical analysis and market practice

- **The Evolution of Technical Analysis** — Andrew W. Lo and Jasmina Hasanhodzic, 2010.  
  Official MIT book listing: https://alo.mit.edu/types/book/

- **The Heretics of Finance** — Andrew W. Lo and Jasmina Hasanhodzic, 2009.  
  Official MIT book listing: https://alo.mit.edu/types/book/  
  Useful for practitioner interviews and market-craft questions such as pattern validation, luck, testing, and market laws.

## 4. Hedge funds, risk management, and systemic risk

### Hedge-fund framework

- **Hedge Funds: An Analytic Perspective** — Andrew W. Lo, Princeton University Press, 2008; revised and expanded edition, 2010.  
  Official MIT book listing: https://alo.mit.edu/types/book/  
  Google Books: https://books.google.com/books/about/Hedge_Funds.html?id=t3N6UYt6IgEC  
  Use for hedge-fund return properties, serial correlation, illiquidity, beta replication, active management, systemic risk, and practical investment process.

### Systemic risk and hedge funds

- **Systemic Risk and Hedge Funds** — Nicholas Chan, Mila Getmansky, Shane Haas, Andrew W. Lo, NBER / The Risks of Financial Institutions, 2007.  
  NBER page: https://www.nber.org/papers/w11200  
  PDF: https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c9611/c9611.pdf  
  Measures include illiquidity exposure, nonlinear factor models, liquidation-probability models, volatility/distress models, and regime switching.

### Survey of systemic-risk measures

- **A Survey of Systemic Risk Analytics** — Dimitrios Bisias, Mark Flood, Andrew W. Lo, Stavros Valavanis, Office of Financial Research Working Paper, 2012.  
  https://www.financialresearch.gov/working-papers/2012/01/05/a-survey-of-systemic-risk-analytics/  
  Use for a taxonomy of systemic-risk measures, data inputs, outputs, and open-source implementation.

### Quant meltdown / crowded liquidation

- **What Happened to the Quants in August 2007? Evidence from Factors and Transactions Data** — Amir Khandani and Andrew W. Lo, Journal of Financial Markets, 2011.  
  MIT PDF: https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/august07.pdf  
  NBER PDF: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14465/w14465.pdf  
  Use for forced liquidation, factor-crowding, market-neutral unwind, and withdrawal of market-making risk capital.

### Financial crisis narratives

- **Reading About the Financial Crisis: A Twenty-One-Book Review** — Andrew W. Lo, Journal of Economic Literature, 2012.  
  AEA page: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.50.1.151  
  MIT PDF: https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/75360/Lo_Reading%20about.pdf  
  Use for multi-narrative crisis thinking and the discipline of not forcing a single explanation.

### Technology-leveraged finance

- **Moore's Law vs. Murphy's Law in the Financial System: Who's Winning?** — Andrew W. Lo, Journal of Investment Management, 2017.  
  Official MIT risk topic page: https://alo.mit.edu/topic/risk-management-and-systemic-risk/  
  Use for technology-enabled finance, flash crashes, cybersecurity, algorithmic errors, robo-advisors, crypto, and systemic risk.

### Stop-loss and dynamic risk management

- **Stop-Loss Strategies with Serial Correlation, Regime Switching, and Transaction Costs** — Andrew W. Lo and Alexander Remorov, Journal of Financial Markets, 2017.  
  Official MIT risk topic page: https://alo.mit.edu/topic/risk-management-and-systemic-risk/  
  Use for state-dependent stop-loss behavior and transaction-cost-aware downside protection.

### TRC networks

- **TRC Networks and Systemic Risk** — Andrew W. Lo and Roger M. Stein, Journal of Alternative Investments, 2016.  
  Official MIT risk topic page: https://alo.mit.edu/topic/risk-management-and-systemic-risk/  
  Use for combining network analysis and tail-risk contribution.

### Credit risk and ML

- **Risk and Risk Management in the Credit Card Industry** — Florentin Butaru, Qingqing Chen, Brian Clark, Sanmay Das, Andrew W. Lo, Akhtar Siddique, Journal of Banking & Finance, 2016.  
  Official MIT risk topic page: https://alo.mit.edu/topic/risk-management-and-systemic-risk/  
  Use for machine learning, credit risk, heterogeneity across institutions, and customized supervision.

### Dynamic indexes

- **What Is an Index?** — Andrew W. Lo, Journal of Portfolio Management, 2016.  
  Official MIT risk topic page: https://alo.mit.edu/topic/risk-management-and-systemic-risk/  
  Use for dynamic index definition, systematic transparency, and decoupling risk management from alpha generation.

## 8. How to use this source map

For each source, extract four things:

1. **Mechanism** — What causal story does the source propose?
2. **Measurement** — What variables or tests does it use?
3. **Failure mode** — What assumptions break?
4. **Trading/risk implication** — How would this change a strategy, portfolio, or risk process?
